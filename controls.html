Every control Paxio gives you, explained

Real screens, not mockups.

Paxio is ten pieces that work together — five controls a parent sets, two dashboards (one for you, one your child sees), a reward for good streaks, a weekly report, and an email digest so you don't have to open the app to stay in the loop. Here's what each one actually looks like.

Parent dashboard

The first screen you see: a pending-request banner if your child's asked for anything, a usage ring for today with a category breakdown, this week's trend against last week, and four one-tap shortcuts (Internet, Sleep timer, App blocker, Content filter) so common changes don't need a trip into Control.

Screen time limit

A daily cap in 15-minute steps, from 30 minutes to 24 hours, with quick presets for the common ones. It counts every app except calls, messages, and anything in the school folder, and resets at midnight. Good streaks earn bonus weekend minutes — but only with your approval, nothing is added automatically.

App blocking

Every installed app, listed with an Allowed/Blocked toggle and its own optional per-app time limit — so a game can be capped at 30 minutes without touching the overall daily limit. Phone and Contacts are always allowed, even once every other limit is hit.

Content filtering

Age presets (Under 7 / 8–10 / Teen / Open) set every category at once, or each of the 15 categories — adult content, gambling, violence, social media, and more — can be toggled individually. It runs as a local, on-device VPN: DNS requests are inspected to block matching domains, but browsing traffic never leaves the device or reaches a Paxio server.

Bedtime schedule

A sleep and wake time, set per day of the week. The screen dims 15 minutes before lights-out as a warning, then everything but calls sleeps until the wake time — a separate mechanism from the daily limit, so a kid who hasn't touched their limit yet still can't be on the phone at 1am.

Internet pause

A master switch plus quick presets — 30 minutes, 1 hour, or "Dinner" — for pausing internet access outright, without touching any other limit. Calls and texts keep working through a pause, since they run over the carrier network, not data. Only works on a child's own dedicated device, not a phone that's shared with a parent.

Child dashboard

What your child sees on their own device: a countdown ring for time left today, a 7-day streak row, and a plain-language "what's on right now" summary — not a list of restrictions to push back against, just today's picture. Switching back to the parent view is PIN-gated, so picking up the phone doesn't hand over the whole dashboard.

Streak bonus

Every 5 clean days — inside the screen time limit, no blocked-app attempts — earns 20 bonus weekend minutes. It's visible to your child as a real reward for a real pattern, and it never applies itself: each one lands in your queue to approve, same as an extra-time request. The 7-day streak row on the child dashboard above is where they see it building.

Reports screen

Daily, Weekly, or Monthly views, each with a usage trend chart and seven stat tiles: screen time, over-limit days, blocked app attempts, days inside the limit, latest phone use, average session length, and requests sent. Free shows 3 stats and 30 days of history; Pro unlocks all seven and a full year.

Weekly notification

A digest lands in your inbox roughly once a week — the same numbers as the Reports screen, without having to open the app to see them. It's opt-in per event type from Settings → Email Notifications, so it's easy to turn off entirely or narrow to just the events you care about.

Also included

  • Parent PIN — every setting on this page sits behind it, and it's what gates switching a shared device back from child view.
  • Uninstall protection — adds a confirmation step before Paxio can be removed from a child's device; a deterrent, not a hard block.
  • Multiple children, synced — each gets their own profile, limits, and controls, synced across devices under one account.
  • Extra-time requests — your child can ask for more time, a bedtime pause, or to unblock an app; each request lands on your Overview screen to approve or deny.
  • Suggested age-based defaults — onboarding proposes a starting screen timer, content filter band, and app blocker setup for your child's age, which you can accept as-is or adjust before saving.
