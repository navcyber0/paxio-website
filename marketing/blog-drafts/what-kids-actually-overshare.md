# What kids are actually sharing on social apps (and why oversharing is the real risk, not the app itself)

_Meta description: The risk on most social apps isn't the app — it's what gets shared through it without a kid realizing the audience or the permanence. Here's what that actually looks like._

_~2 min read_

Parental anxiety about social apps usually focuses on which app it is — this one's fine, that one's risky. In practice, the app matters less than what gets shared through it. Most of what actually causes problems isn't dangerous content — it's ordinary information shared without a clear sense of who can see it or how long it sticks around.

## Location, without realizing it's location

Photos often carry more location information than the visible content suggests — a school uniform, a street sign, embedded metadata some apps still attach by default. A kid posting "at practice" is thinking about sharing a moment, not location disclosure — the information leaks as a side effect, not the point.

## Real-time vs. after-the-fact

Posting "here now" while still there tells anyone watching where *right now*, not just where. Most kids don't distinguish between these two timings when posting, because it's a timing question, not a content-moderation one — not something the app prompts you to think about.

## The audience is bigger than it feels

A post to "friends" includes friends-of-friends reach through shares and screenshots a kid has no visibility into. The felt audience (who they're picturing while typing) and the actual reachable audience (anyone those friends might forward it to) are different numbers — that gap is where most oversharing regret comes from, not posting to strangers on purpose.

## Permanence is the hardest part to explain

"Delete" removes something from the immediate feed, not necessarily from every cache or screenshot it's already been captured into. A kid whose whole interface experience says "delete = gone" needs this said explicitly — it's a conceptual gap, not a technology one.

## What actually helps

Not a blanket "don't post anything" rule — that just pushes sharing into apps you don't know about. More useful: one concrete habit — before posting something with a location or a person in it, ask "would I be okay with this reaching someone outside my friend group." Turning off location metadata by default (rather than trusting a per-post reminder) removes a whole category of accidental disclosure without needing ongoing vigilance.

## Where Paxio fits, and where it doesn't

Content filtering and app blocking control which apps a kid can reach — they don't see what gets posted inside an already-allowed app, a genuinely different kind of monitoring. What does help directly: keeping unsupervised access to higher-risk apps limited, and screen-time visibility as a rough proxy for exposure.
