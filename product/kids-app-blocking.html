The details

How it actually works

Per-app control

Blocking isn't all-or-nothing — each app gets its own setting.

  • Toggle any installed app between Allowed and Blocked in one tap.
  • Give any app its own time limit, separate from the overall daily screen time cap.
  • New apps your child installs show up automatically, defaulting to whatever your household's suggested setting was at onboarding.

What's always allowed

A few things stay reachable no matter what's blocked.

  • Phone and Contacts are always allowed, even once every other limit is hit.
  • Paxio itself is exempt from its own blocking, so settings stay reachable.
  • Blocking is enforced by Android's Accessibility Service, not by uninstalling or disabling apps — nothing is removed from the device.

Enforcement, not detection

A blocked app is stopped before it opens, not flagged after the fact.

  • When a blocked app is opened, Paxio intercepts it immediately with a simple block screen.
  • Blocked-app attempts are logged and show up on the Reports screen, so repeated attempts are visible.
  • There's no way to force-quit or hide from the block — it runs as a system-level accessibility service.

FAQ

App Blocking — common questions

Can my child uninstall a blocked app to get around it?

Uninstall protection adds a confirmation step before Paxio itself can be removed, and app blocking is enforced by Android's Accessibility Service — not by hiding or disabling the app — so uninstalling the blocked app itself doesn't bypass Paxio.

Can I set a time limit on just one app, like a game?

Yes — any app can get its own optional time limit, independent of the overall daily screen time cap.

Will new apps be blocked by default?

New installs default to whatever your household's suggested app setup was during onboarding — you can adjust any app individually afterward.

Can my child see which apps are blocked?

Yes — opening a blocked app shows a clear block screen rather than silently failing, so there's no ambiguity about what happened.
