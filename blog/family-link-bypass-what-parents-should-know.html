Family Link bypass: how kids get around it, and what actually helps

Paxio·October 6, 2026·~4 min read

Your child found a way past Family Link bedtime, or you suspect they will. Before anything else: that is normal, it is rarely a sign of a broken app, and in most cases the gap is in what the controls cover rather than in a clever hack. Here is what Google itself says Family Link does and doesn’t reach, what changed for teens, and what actually helps.

Disclaimer: Google, Qustodio, Kids360 and other apps named here are described from their publicly stated features and help pages as of this writing, not a verified audit. Paxio isn’t affiliated with, sponsored by, or paid by any of them. Features and prices change, so check each vendor’s own page.

Can kids bypass Google Family Link?

A determined, technically capable kid can get around almost any consumer control, and Family Link is no exception. We covered the general picture in can your child bypass parental controls. The useful question is not “is it unbeatable?” but “where does it stop, and have I covered that part?”.

This guide deliberately doesn’t list step-by-step workarounds. It explains the gaps so you can close them.

Where does Family Link not reach?

Google’s own help pages describe several limits worth knowing:

  • Limits are per device. Google says daily limits “apply to each Android device or Chromebook your child uses”, so a 2-hour limit gives 2 hours on each device (Google Family Link Help). A second tablet or an old phone is a separate allowance unless you set it up too.
  • System apps can’t be limited. The same page says system apps can’t have app limits placed on them, and that app limits work on Android 7 and up.
  • Browser settings only apply inside Chrome on a supervised profile. For computers and iPhones or iPads, Google says a child can sign out of Chrome and switch profiles, or use other browsers, where Family Link’s Chrome settings won’t apply (Google Family Link Help).

None of these is a hack. They are the edges of the tool, which is why the next step is to check every device and browser your child can reach.

What changed for teens at 13?

Google’s help page says a child can stop supervision once they reach 13 (or the applicable age in their country), that a child under 18 needs a parent’s approval to stop it, and that you’ll both be notified when it stops (Google Family Link Help).

The approval step follows public criticism in January 2026, when Google had been emailing children nearing 13 about removing supervision themselves. Google then said teens would need explicit parental approval, rolling out globally (Bitdefender). Google can change this again, so check the help page for the current rule.

What actually helps?

  1. Set up every device your child uses, including the old tablet in the drawer, and set the limit on each.
  2. Check which apps the limit really covers. If a system app or browser is where the time goes, a per-app limit won’t help.
  3. Keep your parent credentials separate from anything your child can guess or watch you type.
  4. Review once a month. Open the activity report and look for the pattern, such as one app or one late hour, instead of chasing each incident.
  5. Talk about the rule, not only the tool. A child who understands why bedtime exists is less likely to hunt for a way round it. For teens, see finding the right balance.

Frequently asked questions

How do kids bypass Family Link?

Mostly through gaps in what it covers: a second device with its own allowance, system apps that can't be limited, browsers outside a supervised Chrome profile, or a teen stopping supervision. Check each of those before assuming the app failed.

Will I be notified if my teen stops Family Link supervision?

Google's help page says you will both be notified when supervision is stopped, and that a child under 18 needs a parent's approval to stop it. Google can change this rule, so confirm on its current help page.

Does a Family Link time limit apply on every device?

No. Google says daily limits apply to each Android device or Chromebook your child uses, so a 2-hour limit means 2 hours on each device. Set the limit on every device your child uses.
