Best parental control app in India: what to check before you choose

By Naveenkumar Arulmani, Founder of Paxio·October 6, 2026·Updated October 8, 2026·~5 min read

Short answer: there is no single best parental control app in India. It depends on your child’s phone and what you want to control. For free basics plus location on Android, Google Family Link. For call and text monitoring across Android, iPhone and computers, Qustodio. For screen time, app blocking and content filtering on Android without location or message tracking, Paxio’s free plan. The table below compares them, and the rest of this guide covers what to check before you choose.

What should you look for in a parental control app in India?

  • Android support. Check your family’s phones first. Some apps are Android only, others cover iPhone with fewer controls.
  • Price in rupees. Look for a free tier that is genuinely usable and a paid plan billed in INR, not a dollar price that changes with the exchange rate.
  • Real enforcement. Screen time limits and app blocks that hold, not suggestions your child can ignore.
  • Content filtering that works across every app and browser, not just one browser.
  • A clear privacy policy. What does the app collect about your child, and where does it go?
  • Honesty about limits. No app stops every determined teenager. We explain the realistic ceiling in can your child bypass parental controls.

Why does India’s data law matter when you choose an app?

India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 has a section on children’s data. The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with an eighteen-month period for phased compliance, and the government’s release says verifiable consent from a parent or guardian is required when a child’s personal data is involved, unless the processing relates to essential services such as healthcare, education or real-time safety (Press Information Bureau).

Law-firm summaries of Section 9 say it also bars tracking, behavioural monitoring of children and targeted advertising aimed at them (Tsaaro), and that the children’s-data obligations take effect in May 2027 (SGCMS summary).

We are not lawyers, and how the Act applies to a parent-run control app is a question for one. The practical takeaway for you is simple: ask what an app collects about your child and why, and be cautious about apps that need message content, location or social activity to work.

How do the main options compare for Indian families?

  • Google Family Link: free and from Google, with app approval and location. Its content filtering is shallower than a dedicated filter, and daily limits apply per device. See Paxio vs Family Link.
  • Qustodio: feature-rich, including call and text monitoring, with a more limited free tier. See Paxio vs Qustodio.
  • Kids360: works on Android and iOS, with live location and history and a task-based model where kids earn extra screen time. It uses two apps, Kids360 for the parent and Alli360 for the child, and its Play Store listing shows in-app purchases from $0.99 to $99.99 rather than a plan price. See Paxio vs Kids360.
  • Norton Family: from Norton. Its Play Store data safety section lists location, web browsing history and photos among the data it may collect (checked 3 October 2026). See Norton’s own page for plans and prices.
  • Paxio: Android only. The free plan covers one child, a daily screen time limit, blocking up to 3 apps and a content filter. The Pro plan is ₹499 a month or ₹4,999 a year per our pricing page and adds per-app limits, bedtime schedules and up to 3 children. It has no location tracking and doesn’t read messages or calls.

If your child uses an iPhone, Paxio will not work, because it is Android only. Apple’s built-in Screen Time is free on iPhone and iPad.

Which one should you pick?

If you want free, simple basics and location, Family Link is hard to beat. If you want message and call monitoring, Qustodio does that and Paxio deliberately does not. If you want screen time, app blocking and content filtering without watching your child’s messages, and you are on Android, try Paxio’s free plan. Most of these have a free option, so test one for a week with your child before paying for anything.

Why does a parental control app stop working on some Android phones?

In our own testing on a Vivo phone, Paxio’s accessibility service switched itself off. The cause was a setting in the app’s App Info page, “Remove permissions and free up space”, which is Android’s cleanup for apps you haven’t opened for a while. Turning that setting off fixed it. On a ColorOS phone the same option was labelled “Manage app if unused” and was greyed out and off, so the name and availability vary by brand.

The same setting can affect any app that relies on an accessibility service, not just Paxio. If a parental control app stops blocking after a few weeks on a Vivo, Oppo, Xiaomi or similar phone, check that setting first, and check the battery restrictions on the app as well.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free parental control app in India?

Yes. Google Family Link is free, and Paxio has a free plan for one child with a daily screen time limit, blocking for up to 3 apps and a content filter. Paid plans add more children, per-app limits and schedules.

Which parental control app works without tracking my child's location?

Paxio never tracks location, and it doesn't read messages or calls. Some other apps include location as a core feature, so check each app's feature list and privacy policy before you install.

Does India have a law on children's data and parental control apps?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 has a section on children's data. The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 with an eighteen-month period for phased compliance, and require verifiable parent or guardian consent when a child's personal data is involved, with exceptions such as healthcare, education and real-time safety. Summaries put the children's-data obligations in force from May 2027. How it applies to a specific app is a legal question, so ask each vendor what it collects and why.

Why does my parental control app stop working on a Vivo or Oppo phone?

On a Vivo phone we found that Android's unused-app cleanup, shown as "Remove permissions and free up space" in the app's App Info page, switched off Paxio's accessibility service. Turning that setting off fixed it. The option can be named differently on other brands, for example "Manage app if unused" on a ColorOS phone.
