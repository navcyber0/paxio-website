Before your child's phone goes missing: a checklist to set up now

By Naveenkumar Arulmani, Founder of Paxio·October 25, 2026·~4 min read

The best time to deal with a lost phone is before it happens. Set a screen lock, turn on Google’s Find Hub and 2-Step Verification, keep the phone’s IMEI and purchase bill somewhere safe, and agree a no-blame rule with your child so they tell you straight away.

This is general guidance for parents of Android phones. Settings and official processes change over time, so check the current steps with Google, your mobile operator and the relevant authorities when you need them.

Why does a lost phone matter more for a child?

A child’s phone usually holds their photos, chats, school accounts and game logins, and it may stay signed in to all of them. Whoever finds it can see notifications on the lock screen, and a child who is afraid of punishment may wait before telling you. Every hour of delay gives you fewer options.

What should you set up on the phone now?

  1. A screen lock. Set a PIN, pattern or password, so a finder or thief cannot simply open the phone.
  2. Find Hub. Google’s Find Hub can ring, locate, secure and erase a lost Android phone, but only if the phone is signed in to a Google Account, location is on and Find Hub is turned on (Google support). Set it up now, because it cannot be switched on after the phone has gone.
  3. 2-Step Verification on the Google Account. Google says you must turn it on to lock or erase a lost device remotely. Our guide to strong passwords and 2-step login walks through it.
  4. Hidden lock-screen notifications. Set the phone to hide message content on the lock screen, so a finder cannot read private messages or one-time passwords.
  5. Backups. Make sure photos and contacts are backed up, so losing the phone does not mean losing everything on it.

What records should you keep?

  • The IMEI. This is the number that identifies the handset itself. You can see it by dialling *#06# on the phone, and it is usually printed on the box and the purchase bill. Write it down now and keep it where you can reach it without the phone.
  • The purchase bill or a photo of it, and a photo of the box label.
  • The mobile number and operator for the SIM in the phone.
  • A list of the important accounts the phone is signed in to, so you know what to change if it is lost.
  • Backup codes for 2-Step Verification, kept by a parent, not on the phone.

What should your family plan say?

Agree it before anything goes wrong, and keep it short:

  • Tell a parent straight away if the phone is missing. Nobody will be punished for losing it.
  • Do not try to chase or confront anyone to get it back.
  • The parent’s first steps are to try Find Hub, sign the phone out of accounts from another device, change important passwords, and contact the mobile operator.
  • If it looks stolen, the parent will contact the police.

That no-blame rule matters most. A child who fears punishment may hide the loss, and every hour of delay makes the other steps harder.

Where can you find the official process in India?

India has an official government portal, Sanchar Saathi, for dealing with lost or stolen handsets. We do not reproduce its steps here because they can change. Check the current process on the official site and ask your mobile operator. Keeping the IMEI and the bill ready now will save time later.

Where does a tool like Paxio fit?

Paxio does not track location, so it cannot find a lost phone. Use Find Hub for that. When your child gets a replacement phone, install Paxio on it and set the controls up again.

Frequently asked questions

What is an IMEI number, and where do I find it?

The IMEI identifies your phone handset. You can usually see it by dialling *#06# on the phone, and it is often printed on the box and the purchase bill. Save it somewhere that does not depend on the phone.

Do I need 2-Step Verification to lock a lost Android phone remotely?

Google says you must turn on 2-Step Verification to lock or erase a lost Android device remotely, and Find Hub must be set up in advance. Do both now, not after the phone is lost.

Should I be angry if my child loses their phone?

Try not to be. Children who fear punishment often delay telling you, which makes recovery harder. A calm, no-blame response gets you the information you need quickly.
